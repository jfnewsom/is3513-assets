Document Standards

Module 1: Threat Intelligence & Reconnaissance

Overview

Welcome to NEXUS Security Consulting. Before you touch a client engagement, you need to be able to produce a document that holds together. Every Engagement Packet you submit this term is graded partly on mechanics — whether your Table of Contents is accurate, whether your figures are numbered and referenced, whether your sources are cited. Those points were the single largest avoidable loss last term, and none of them had anything to do with security knowledge. This lab teaches the mechanics once, on a sandbox document you cannot break in any way that matters, and then tests them on a quiz you can take as many times as you like.

“Nobody outside this building sees your terminal. They see the report. I have watched excellent technical work get dismissed because the Table of Contents was full of screenshot filenames and half the figures were numbered by hand. Learn the tools once and you never think about it again.”

Maya Rodriguez
Senior Penetration Tester, NEXUS Security

  • Confirm your computer can host the Kali VM that every later lab depends on

Key Concepts

  • Heading styles, and what the Table of Contents actually collects
  • Automatic figure captions and cross-references
  • Word’s citation manager and the reference list it builds for you
  • Document properties as your identity on a file

Pre-Flight — Can Your Machine Run This Course?

What You’re Doing

Checking four things about the computer you intend to use this term: how much memory it has, how much free disk it has, what kind of processor it has, and whether virtualization is switched on.

Why It Matters

Lab 1.1 asks you to build a Kali Linux virtual machine, and every lab after it runs inside that VM. If your machine cannot host one, you need to know in week 1 while there is still time to do something about it — not the night Lab 1.1 is due. This takes five minutes and it is the only part of this lab that is about hardware.

  1. Check your memory. Windows: Task Manager > Performance > Memory. Mac: Apple menu > About This Mac.
  2. Check your free disk space. Windows: Settings > System > Storage. Mac: Apple menu > System Settings > General > Storage.
  3. Check your processor type. Windows: Task Manager > Performance > CPU, or Settings > System > About. Mac: About This Mac — look for ‘Chip: Apple M1/M2/M3/M4’ versus ‘Processor: Intel’. This determines which Kali image you download, so do not skip it.
  4. Windows only: confirm virtualization is enabled. Task Manager > Performance > CPU. Look for ‘Virtualization: Enabled’ on the right. If it says Disabled, it has to be turned on in your BIOS/UEFI before VirtualBox can run a VM at all. Search the web for “enable virtualization” plus your computer’s make and model — the key you press at boot (often F2, F10, Del or Esc) and the name of the setting (VT-x, AMD-V, SVM Mode, Intel Virtualization Technology) both differ by manufacturer, so there is no single instruction that covers everyone.
  5. Take a screenshot of each check. Windows: press Windows + Shift + S and drag a box around the panel. The snip goes to your clipboard, and a notification appears in the bottom-right — click it, then press Ctrl + S to save the file. If that feels fiddly, press Windows + PrtScn instead: it grabs the whole screen and writes a .png straight into Pictures > Screenshots with nothing further to do.
  6. Mac: press Command + Shift + 4, then drag a box around the panel. The .png lands on your Desktop by itself. Command + Shift + 3 grabs the whole screen if you would rather not aim.
  7. Confirm you have four saved files, not four clipboard copies. This is the step people skip. A snip sitting on the clipboard disappears the moment you copy anything else, and Insert > Pictures needs a file on disk. Give them names you will recognise — memory, disk, processor, virtualization.
  8. Write the four answers down. If any of them fall short, act on it today — see below.

What Lab 1.1 Will Ask For

Memory The Kali VM is allocated 4096 MB minimum, 8192 MB recommended. Your machine has to run that and your normal desktop at the same time. 8 GB total is workable at the 4096 MB setting. Below that, talk to me in week 1.

Open the Sandbox and Turn On the Navigation Pane

What You’re Doing

Downloading the Lab 1.0 sandbox document, opening it in desktop Word, and turning on the one panel that tells you whether your document is healthy.

Why It Matters

The Navigation Pane and the Table of Contents read from the same source: heading styles. Anything wrong in the Pane will be wrong in the Table of Contents. Keeping it open while you work is the difference between catching a problem in ten seconds and hunting it for an hour.

lightbulb
Download the Sandbox

IS3513_Lab1.0_Sandbox.docx — open it in desktop Word. Nothing you do to this file is submitted or graded. Break it on purpose.

  1. Download the sandbox document: IS3513_Lab1.0_Sandbox.docx. Save it somewhere you can find it again.
  2. Open it in desktop Microsoft Word. Not a browser, not Google Docs, not Preview.
  3. Maximise the Word window. In a narrow window Word collapses the ribbon to unlabelled icons and every instruction below becomes a scavenger hunt.
  4. Turn on the Navigation Pane. Windows: View > Navigation Pane (tick the checkbox). Mac: View > Sidebar > Navigation.
  5. Leave it open for the whole lab.
check_circle

The sandbox open in desktop Word with a panel down the left side listing the document’s sections. You should be able to click an entry and jump to it.

3
Read the Four Pairs

What You’re Doing

Reading through the sandbox’s demonstration section, where each of the four mechanics appears twice — once done correctly and once done the way that costs points.

Why It Matters

You are not being asked to memorise rules in the abstract. Each pair puts the right way and the wrong way next to each other so you can see the difference in the document itself, and in the Navigation Pane, rather than being told about it.

  1. Scroll to Appendix B — Additional Screenshots. The four pairs live there.
  2. Pair 1, Captions. Compare the correct caption against the one styled as Heading 2. Watch the Navigation Pane as you scroll past them — only one of them shows up as a section.
  3. Pair 2, The blank heading. Find the blank entry in the Navigation Pane. There is an empty paragraph carrying a heading style, and it is invisible in the document body.
  4. Pair 3, Cross-references. One says Figure 1 because Word computed it. The other says Figure 1 because somebody typed it. They look identical right now.
  5. Pair 4, Citations. Same story — one is a field, one is text. Click each one. The real citation highlights grey when you click it; typed text does not.
lightbulb
Pro Tip

Clicking is the fastest way to tell a field from typed text. Fields shade grey when the cursor enters them. If you are ever unsure whether something in your own packet is real, click it.

search
Dig Deeper

Press Alt+F9 on Windows, or Option+F9 on Mac, to toggle field codes on and off. The document will suddenly show you the machinery behind every caption, cross-reference and citation. Press it again to go back.

checkpoint
4
Repair the Five Planted Faults

What You’re Doing

Fixing every fault in the sandbox, using Word’s built-in tools rather than formatting things by hand.

Why It Matters

This is the whole lab. Every one of these five faults appeared in real Engagement Packets last term, and every one of them cost points that had nothing to do with the quality of the security work.

  1. Fix the blank heading. Click in the empty paragraph flagged in Pair 2, then apply Home > Styles > Normal. The blank entry disappears from the Navigation Pane.
  2. Fix the heading-styled caption. Click in the caption line in Pair 1 that is styled Heading 2. Apply Home > Styles > Normal, then References > Insert Caption to make it a real caption.
  3. Insert one of your hardware screenshots. Put the cursor on the empty line under the third placeholder in Pair 1, then Windows: Insert > Pictures > This Device. Mac: Insert > Pictures > Picture from File. Pick any of the four you captured in Checkpoint 1 — the memory one is a good choice. This is the same move you will make on every Engagement Packet for the rest of the term.
  4. Caption the image you just inserted. Click on the line below it — on its own line, not at the end of the image’s paragraph — then References > Insert Caption. Accept the Figure label and type a short description after the number. Do not type the word ‘Figure’ yourself; the dialog supplies it, and that is the whole point — Word owns the number, so it renumbers when things move.
  5. Replace the typed cross-reference. Delete the hand-typed ‘Figure 1’ in Pair 3 and use References > Cross-reference. Set Reference type to Figure, Insert reference to ‘Only label and number’, pick the figure, and Insert.
  6. Replace the typed citation. Delete the hand-typed citation in Pair 4. Use References > Insert Citation > Add New Source, fill in the source, and insert it.
warning
Common Pitfalls
  • Do not drag-select a heading line. Click in it. If you highlight the heading plus the blank line under it, Word styles both paragraphs and you have created a new blank entry while fixing the old one.
  • In the Create Source dialog, the URL box is hidden until you tick ‘Show All Bibliography Fields’. Tick it first or you will not be able to enter a web source properly.
  • Insert Caption places the caption where your cursor is. Put the cursor on its own line below the figure before you run it.
check_circle
What You Should Have

A Navigation Pane with no blank entries and no figure captions in it. Only real sections should be listed.

5
Update Every Field and Verify

What You’re Doing

Refreshing every field in the document, then checking your work the way you should check every packet before you submit it.

Why It Matters

Fields do not update themselves. A Table of Contents built before you finished writing is a Table of Contents that describes a document you no longer have. This step takes five seconds and is the most commonly skipped step in the entire course.

  1. Select the whole document and update every field. Windows: Ctrl+A then F9. Mac: Command+A then F9.
  2. If nothing happens, your keyboard’s function keys are set to media controls. Hold Fn as well, or use the Fn Lock key. This is common on laptops, Windows and Mac alike.
  3. Right-click the Table of Contents and choose Update Field, then Update entire table. Not ‘page numbers only’ — that will not pick up sections you added or removed.
  4. Read the Navigation Pane top to bottom. No blank entries. No captions. No dates or hour counts.
  5. Read the Table of Contents. It should match the Navigation Pane exactly.
  6. Add a figure above your first captioned figure and update fields again. Watch the caption numbers and the cross-reference renumber themselves. That is what you bought by doing it properly.
check_circle
What You Should Have

A Table of Contents and a Navigation Pane that agree with each other and contain only real sections. Figure numbering that survives you inserting a figure in the middle.

lightbulb
Pro Tip

Make Ctrl+A then F9 the last thing you do before saving any packet, every time, forever. It costs five seconds and it is the single highest-value habit in this course.

6
Set Your Document Identity

What You’re Doing

Setting the two document properties that identify a Word file as yours, and practising it here where it does not count.

Why It Matters

Your work in this course is identified by your abc123, not by your filename. Later on it also has to match the kali-abc123 hostname visible in your screenshots — together they are how a document is tied to the machine that produced its evidence. Set it here once so the habit is already in place.

  1. Open document properties. Windows: File > Info. Mac: File > Properties.
  2. Add your abc123 to the Author field. Windows: the Author box on the right of the Info pane. Mac: the Summary tab.
  3. Word appends your abc123 to the existing author rather than replacing it. That is expected. Leave it.
  4. Set the abc123 custom property. Windows: File > Info > Properties > Advanced Properties > Custom. Mac: File > Properties > Custom.
  5. Save the file.
warning
Your abc123, Not the Placeholder

Use your own abc123 everywhere it appears — in document properties and, from Lab 1.1 onward, in your VM hostname. Students who left the literal text ‘abc123’ in place last term had packets that could not be matched to them.

Both Sides of the Shield
security
What Red Team Learns
  • The Report Is the Deliverable: A client never watches you work. The engagement is judged entirely on the document that lands on their desk, which makes document control an operational skill, not an administrative one.
  • Evidence Has to Point Somewhere: A finding that references ‘the screenshot below’ instead of a numbered figure falls apart the moment anything is reordered. Cross-references keep claims attached to their proof.
  • Credibility Is Cheap to Lose: A figure number that does not match the figure tells a client that nobody checked the document. They will wonder what else nobody checked.
security
What Blue Team Learns
  • Reproducibility: A runbook whose screenshots are not paired with the commands that produced them cannot be followed by the next analyst. Documentation that only makes sense to its author is not documentation.
  • Chain of Custody: Tying a document to the host that produced its evidence — abc123 in the properties, kali-abc123 in the screenshots — is the same discipline that makes incident evidence defensible.
  • Auditable Sourcing: A reference list generated from real citations can be verified. One typed by hand cannot be distinguished from one invented.
sync_alt
The Purple Team Connection

Offence and defence produce the same artifact: a document somebody else has to act on. The mechanics in this lab are what make that document trustworthy, and they are identical whether you are reporting an attack path or an incident.
